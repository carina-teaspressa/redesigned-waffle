BFCM 2025 Campaign Performance

What worked, what didn't, and what's worth reviving — built from the Klaviyo/Postscript export and the Q4 Shopify/ads reporting workbook. Updated with your clarifications on discounting setup and data scope.
Scope: DTC channel only, Draft Orders (large corporate/custom buyers) excluded per your note. Core BFCM window = Nov 24–Dec 1, 2025, compared against the rest of Q4 (Oct 1–Dec 31) and against BFCM 2024 where the data allows it.

Highlights

  • Site conversion rate nearly doubled during BFCM week (2.7% vs. 1.5% for the rest of the quarter), peaking at 4.7% on Black Friday.
  • BFCM revenue was up ~42% year-over-year, but order count was flat (233 vs. 238 in 2024) —
  • AOV climbed from ~$58 to ~$83, largely on Kit sales.
  • Dec 1–2 were the two biggest revenue days of the entire quarter.

Luxe Mixer Kits are driving AOV up

Every top-10 product by gross sales during the quarter is a Luxe Mixer kit (Candy Cane Cookie Latte Cafe Set, Cozy Cabin Coffee Trio, Champagne Bellini Mixer Kit, etc.) rather than a single item. That product mix, combined with the spend-tiered GIFTMORE discount, is the most plausible driver of AOV growth outpacing order growth.

So what: The AOV win is a merchandising/bundling result as much as a marketing-channel. Worth protecting and expanding regardless of which channel gets credit for the click.

The sequenced discount ladder lines up with the sales pattern

CHEERS10 + GIFTMORE (10–30% by spend) built through the week before Black Friday; SIPTHEWEEKEND's flat 30% off landed for the Cyber Weekend itself — and is the single largest discount code in Q4 by dollars applied. Gross sales by day: Nov 28 (Black Friday) = $3,653; Dec 1 (Cyber Monday) = $5,621; Dec 2 = $5,653.

So what: escalating from smaller, spend-based discounts into one flagship flat-rate weekend offer is working — the biggest offer landed on the biggest days. Worth keeping this sequencing rather than leading with the deepest discount too early.

New-customer acquisition was strong during BFCM

189 new vs. 42 returning customers placed orders during the core window (82% new) — consistent with the paid/organic search mix (Google search + cross-network drove the largest share of Nov–Dec revenue in GA).

So what: BFCM did its job as an acquisition event. The open question is what happens to these ~189 new customers next — see the flows gap below.

Keep the sequenced discount ladder

Give & Get → Buy More Save More → flat 30% flagship weekend offer lined up with the best sales days of the quarter. Worth repeating the sequencing rather than leading with the deepest discount too early in the cycle.

Give Cyber Monday / "final hours" its own hero moment, not a follow-up slot

It outsold Black Friday day-of; the send calendar and paid spend should reflect that going forward.

Keep corporate/custom orders reporting separate

Now that Draft Orders are excluded, worth keeping a habit of pulling DTC-only, channel-filtered exports for future reporting so this cleanup doesn't have to happen after the fact.

Grow the loyalty (Rivo) segment ahead of the next sale

Loyalty-only pre-sale emails converted despite tiny list sizes (12–680 recipients per tier) — the offer works, the audience is just small.

Gaps to FixAutomation, tracking, list health

Lifecycle flows were essentially dormant during the biggest week of the year

Abandoned Cart, Browse Abandonment, and Winback flows show 1–20 recipients per day (vs. thousands for one-off campaigns) and near-zero placed orders across the entire BFCM window. Several flow messages are flagged "deleted" or "draft" in the export —

So what: the highest-intent shoppers of the year — people who added to cart and left — don't look like they were being systematically recovered. Worth confirming directly in Klaviyo whether these were paused on purpose.

Attribution infrastructure needs the planned rebuild

UTM tracking wasn't implemented well this cycle. Klaviyo, GA, and the Shopify referrer report all tell a different size of story for email/SMS revenue, and none of them can be fully trusted in isolation right now.

So what: good news is this is already flagged for cleanup. Worth prioritizing before the next major sale so next year's report can state real channel ROI instead of a range.

SMS cadence shows fatigue signals

Near-daily BFCM sends (7 campaigns in 11 days to a list of ~1,300–1,400) carried unsubscribe rates of 0.4–1.3% per send, and two sends ("black friday 4," "black friday 7," "cyber monday 2") generated unsubscribes with $0 attributed revenue.

So what: the list is small; losing subscribers on non-converting sends compounds against future reach. Worth trimming the lowest-performing sends from the cadence next cycle.
