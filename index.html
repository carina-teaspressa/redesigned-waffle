ClarifiedData notes before the analysis

Draft Orders excluded — corrects the Q4 top line

Draft Orders was the largest single "channel" in the raw export at $80,022 for Q4, ahead of Online Store's $78,252 — you confirmed these are large corporate/custom buyers, not core DTC. Excluding them, true online DTC gross for Q4 is ~$96,030, not the $176,052 blended total.

Limitation: the day-by-day sales trend, the conversion-rate report, and the discount tables below come from Shopify exports that blend all channels together and don't carry a channel field to filter by. So while the Q4 total above is now clean, the BFCM-window and daily figures elsewhere in this report may still include some Draft Order dollars. Flagging this rather than presenting false precision — worth pulling a channel-filtered export next time if a fully clean BFCM-week number matters.

Discount ladder was intentional, not leaking

Confirmed structure: Give & Get (tiered loyalty referral program, always-on), Buy More Save More (GIFTMORE10/20/30, 10–30% by cart size), and a flat sale + GWP layer. CHEERS10 + GIFTMORE ran the week leading into Black Friday to build momentum; the flagship 30%-off SIPTHEWEEKEND ("Love & Sip the Weekend") was reserved for the Cyber Weekend itself.

By the numbers: SIPTHEWEEKEND is the single largest discount code in Q4 by dollars applied ($2,814.60 across 114 orders, $9,382 in gross sales it touched) and GIFTMORE's three tiers combined drove 58 discounted orders. That's consistent with an escalating, sequenced offer — not a flat promo running the same depth all week.

The exports provided don't timestamp discount-code usage, so the original read (discount rate looked heavier outside the BFCM date range than inside it) can't be confirmed or ruled out at the code level with this data — treating your description of the sequencing as the source of truth here rather than re-asserting the earlier "gap."

Email/SMS revenue: real, but the size is unresolved either way

UTM tracking wasn't implemented well this cycle and is being cleaned up for next year. That cuts both ways: Klaviyo's own attribution window may be too generous (crediting orders to a campaign that weren't really driven by it), while GA and the Shopify referrer report almost certainly undercount email/SMS because the links weren't tagged — so those sessions fall into "Direct." True email/SMS-driven revenue for BFCM most likely sits somewhere between Klaviyo's ~$1,950 and GA's ~$215, and is probably closer to the Klaviyo number than the GA one, but neither should be treated as exact until tagging is fixed.

Executive summary

  • Sales lift was real and it was a conversion story, not just a traffic story. Site conversion rate nearly doubled during BFCM week (2.7% vs. 1.5% for the rest of the quarter), peaking at 4.7% on Black Friday.
  • Basket size did the heavy lifting. BFCM revenue was up ~42% year-over-year, but order count was flat (233 vs. 238 in 2024) — the gain came from AOV climbing to ~$83 from ~$58, largely on bundled mixer-kit sales.
  • Cyber Monday (and the day after) outsold Black Friday itself. Dec 1–2 were the two biggest revenue days of the entire quarter, ahead of Nov 28 — and that lines up with SIPTHEWEEKEND being the flagship 30%-off Cyber Weekend offer, sequenced after a smaller pre-BF push.
  • The discount ladder (Give & Get → Buy More Save More → SIPTHEWEEKEND) was a deliberate escalation into the big weekend, not undisciplined discounting — confirmed this cycle.
  • Email/SMS attribution can't be trusted precisely yet — UTM tracking wasn't solid this cycle and is being rebuilt for next year.
  • Automated flows (abandoned cart, browse abandonment, winback) were effectively dark during the highest-intent week of the year.

Bundled "mixer kit" sets are the AOV engine

Every top-10 product by gross sales during the quarter is a bundled kit (Candy Cane Cookie Latte Cafe Set, Cozy Cabin Coffee Trio, Champagne Bellini Mixer Kit, etc.) rather than a single item. That product mix, combined with the spend-tiered GIFTMORE discount, is the most plausible driver of AOV growth outpacing order growth.

So what: the AOV win is a merchandising/bundling result as much as a marketing-channel one — worth protecting and expanding regardless of which channel gets credit for the click.

The sequenced discount ladder lines up with the sales pattern

CHEERS10 + GIFTMORE (10–30% by spend) built through the week before Black Friday; SIPTHEWEEKEND's flat 30% off landed for the Cyber Weekend itself — and is the single largest discount code in Q4 by dollars applied. Gross sales by day: Nov 28 (Black Friday) = $3,653; Dec 1 (Cyber Monday) = $5,621; Dec 2 = $5,653.

So what: escalating from smaller, spend-based discounts into one flagship flat-rate weekend offer is working — the biggest offer landed on the biggest days. Worth keeping this sequencing rather than leading with the deepest discount too early.

New-customer acquisition was strong during BFCM

189 new vs. 42 returning customers placed orders during the core window (82% new) — consistent with the paid/organic search mix (Google search + cross-network drove the largest share of Nov–Dec revenue in GA).

So what: BFCM did its job as an acquisition event. The open question is what happens to these ~189 new customers next — see the flows gap below.

Underperformed / GapsAutomation, tracking, list health

Lifecycle flows were essentially dormant during the biggest week of the year

Abandoned Cart, Browse Abandonment, and Winback flows show 1–20 recipients per day (vs. thousands for one-off campaigns) and near-zero placed orders across the entire BFCM window. Several flow messages are flagged "deleted" or "draft" in the export — you flagged you're not sure why, so this is worth a direct check inside Klaviyo rather than reading too much into the export alone.

So what: the highest-intent shoppers of the year — people who added to cart and left — don't look like they were being systematically recovered. Worth confirming directly in Klaviyo whether these were paused on purpose.

Attribution infrastructure needs the planned rebuild

UTM tracking wasn't implemented well this cycle. Klaviyo, GA, and the Shopify referrer report all tell a different size of story for email/SMS revenue, and none of them can be fully trusted in isolation right now.

So what: good news is this is already flagged for cleanup. Worth prioritizing before the next major sale so next year's report can state real channel ROI instead of a range.

SMS cadence shows fatigue signals

Near-daily BFCM sends (7 campaigns in 11 days to a list of ~1,300–1,400) carried unsubscribe rates of 0.4–1.3% per send, and two sends ("black friday 4," "black friday 7," "cyber monday 2") generated unsubscribes with $0 attributed revenue.

So what: the list is small; losing subscribers on non-converting sends compounds against future reach. Worth trimming the lowest-performing sends from the cadence next cycle.
